The risk score is a ranking score between 0 and 1. It is not an exact probability of default.
A risk score below 0.20 means low risk. Routine yearly monitoring is enough.
A risk score from 0.20 to 0.43 means medium risk. Review the company in the next quarterly cycle.
A risk score of 0.43 or higher means high risk and the company is flagged. A credit analyst must do a manual review before any decision.
The model finds about 6 in 10 real defaults, so a low score does not guarantee that a company is safe. Always check the financial statements too.
If many financial fields are missing, the score is less reliable. Ask for updated financial statements before relying on it.
The score alone must never be used to approve or reject a company. The final decision is always made by a human.
When explaining a score, mention the most important financial indicators and avoid claiming that the company will definitely default.
